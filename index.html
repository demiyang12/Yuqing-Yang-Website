What I Do

As a GIS analyst and spatial data specialist, I build the infrastructure that turns raw geographic data into something decision-makers can act on — integrating street networks, crash records, census tables, satellite imagery, and administrative boundaries into clean, analysis-ready datasets, then building the models and tools that sit on top of them. My work spans predictive modeling, spatial data management, and interactive web mapping, with a particular focus on transportation: speed behavior, crash risk, and network safety.

I'm drawn to problems where rigorous spatial analysis can shift how cities allocate resources — traffic safety, transit accessibility, housing equity. I've collaborated with Philadelphia's Office of Transportation and Infrastructure Systems (OTIS) on Vision Zero research, and published work at the 2024 Annual National Planning Conference. A background in urban planning and design gives me fluency in how the data connects to policy and place.

Where I Come From

I grew up in China and trained as an urban planner at Shenzhen University (Bachelor of Engineering in Urban and Rural Planning, 2025). Shenzhen, a city that grew from fishing village to megacity in 40 years, shaped how I think about rapid urbanization and the gap between planning vision and lived reality.

I completed my Master of Urban Spatial Analytics (MUSA) at Penn's Weitzman School of Design in May 2026, where I gained fluency in both the computational methods and the American planning context.
